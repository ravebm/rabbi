## What the scout listens for

The unit of discovery is a human need expressed in an actual conversation: strained relationships, grief, loneliness, rest, uncertainty, control, belonging, aging, prayer or beginning again. Topics are starting search categories, not verified trends.

Use both public discovery and a small source roster. Candidate sources to verify through their official sites before recording handles: Ram Dass / Love Serve Remember, Be Here Now Network, Tara Brach, Jack Kornfield, Sharon Salzberg, Pema Chodron's official organization, the Rabbi Sacks Legacy, Jewish educators and Hebrew/Bible educators. Include smaller accounts and reader questions, not just celebrity feeds. These are proposed research sources, not endorsements of every teaching or claims of current activity.

The X sample cannot represent all readers. Supplement it with Substack/YouTube where accessible and with aggregate themes from our own authorized reader feedback. Prefer an honest limited sample to a claim about what everyone is feeling.

A quote's popularity is discovery evidence, not authenticity. Verify the original book, talk or official transcript before proposing publication. An unattributed quote can inspire a research question, but must stay unverified. Distinguish an estate repost of an old talk from a newly delivered teaching.

## What counts as evidence

For every candidate preserve the canonical URL, creator, publication timestamp, observation timestamp/timezone, post age, observed metric counts and what could not be observed. Capture search terms and which sources were covered. Deduplicate reposts and repeated quotes.

Use three honest labels:

- **Observed outlier:** numerical evidence relative to the same account and comparable format/post ages, with the baseline links, sample size and method. A high raw count alone is not an outlier.
- **Recurring conversation:** the same need appears across at least three independently authored posts; list the evidence. Three is a pilot selection rule, not statistical proof of a broad trend.
- **Editorial lead:** useful idea, insufficient trend evidence. Include only when especially strong and label it plainly.

Start with the last seven days for relevance. Where a comparable baseline cannot be established, do not invent one or compute a misleading growth rate. Repeated observations can establish change over time; a single snapshot cannot. Separate thoughtful response from outrage, mockery, controversy and large-account reach.

## Turn discovery into original work

For each lead ask: What does this audience need? What can Evan say with authority? Which publication can fulfill that promise? A Hebrew connection must arise from verified meaning and tradition. Do not attach a Hebrew word to a meditation trend simply to fill a slot. The right answer may be Already Home only, or neither.

A shared human concern does not make Jewish and Buddhist/Hindu teachings interchangeable. Preserve each publication's voice and the source tradition's meaning. Study effective formats and questions without copying another writer's wording or distinctive story.
