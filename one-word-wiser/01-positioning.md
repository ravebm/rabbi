# 01 · Positioning

## The name

Keep **One Word Wiser**. It is already the publication name and describes an accessible daily habit. The description and About page carry the explicit Hebrew and Bible promise. A Christian, Jewish, or curious reader should understand who the letter is for without decoding a new product name.

## The tagline

Current: *One Hebrew word, one rabbinic teaching, and one wiser way to live.*

**Keep it.** It does three jobs: it contains "Hebrew," it announces the rabbi, and — this matters — it describes the movement of the letter. It does not require withholding every practical insight or rabbinic source from free readers.

Short form for the header / social bio: **Hebrew for Christians (and curious Jews), from a rabbi.**

## The description (approved — use verbatim)

For the Substack short description, the website, and the Facebook ad landing page:

> The Bible wasn't written in English. One Hebrew word a day, and the verses it opens — Genesis to the Gospels. From Rabbi Evan Moffic. No Hebrew required.

Why it works: the first sentence is the hook and shows first on Substack's discover page; "Genesis to the Gospels" signals both testaments without naming Jesus; the exact send time is left to the About and welcome copy; "No Hebrew required" removes the one objection that stops people subscribing. Don't add "discover," "wisdom," or "journey" to it.

## The logo

The *aleph* — first letter, the number one, and silent: the letter that says nothing and holds everything (that's a Week 1 or Week 4 post: "Why an aleph?"). Deep navy (#1B2A41) on white. Use the same navy and serif for the *Seven Words* PDF so everything reads as one thing.

- **Avatar / favicon:** the small navy square with the cream aleph. The byline is unreadable at avatar size anyway.
- **Header / cover / email masthead:** the full wordmark with "with Rabbi Evan Moffic."
- Check the icon cut at 64px — the aleph's upper-right stroke is detached and can read as a floating *yud*; thicken the joint on the icon version only if it does.

## The positioning statement (internal)

For readers who want to understand the Bible through Hebrew and Jewish interpretation, One Word Wiser offers a short daily letter from Rabbi Evan Moffic. His training, experience teaching in churches, and pastoral judgment connect a passage with a real question in everyday life.

Hebrew for Christians demonstrates interest in this subject, but its public subscriber count does not reveal paid conversion. Its paid kit already includes study materials and rabbinic context. Do not describe it as vocabulary only or claim nobody else can teach Jewish sources. Our testable distinction is Evan’s selection, explanation, and pastoral voice in a simpler reading experience.

## Voice rules for this publication

Carried over from the pulpit voice, adapted for the inbox:

- **Short declaratives. Fragments welcome.** "Soft. Open. It ends with a breath."
- **Direct address.** "Say it with me." "Think about the last time you…"
- **Hebrew always transliterated and italicized, glossed in the same breath.** *Teshuvah*, return. Show the Hebrew script once, at the top, large. Then transliteration only.
- **One main anchor per daily reflection.** Rashi, the Midrash, the Talmud, Heschel, Sacks, Maimonides. Told as a story, with a clear source name and a link when useful. Never a pile-up.
- **Rhythm through repetition.** The word is the drumbeat. Repeat it naturally; never meet a repetition quota.
- **Warm, never cute. Respectful, never preachy.** You are not converting anyone in either direction. You are a rabbi handing a Christian a key to their own Bible.
- **Hebrew Bible and New Testament.** Those are the two names, every time. Not "Old Testament," not "the Christian Bible," not "the Greek Bible," and never "your Bible": the reader and the writer share the book. Say "the English" or "the translation" when the point is the wording.
- **The Hebrew Bible is home.** Most posts never leave it. When a Gospel echo earns its place, Jesus is quoted accurately and warmly. Never "the Christian Bible says." Say "Matthew," "Luke," "Jesus."
- **Translation policy.** Every verse is quoted from the NIV, Hebrew Bible and New Testament alike, then one line names the Hebrew under the English ("The word under 'return' is *shuvah*"), then the transliterated Hebrew phrase with the word bolded. No "my translation." When the NIV's wording hides the word, that gap is the teaching; say it plainly ("the English says *repent*"). Never mix translations inside one post.
- **Honest differences in interpretation.** Explain the relevant difference briefly, accurately, and without pretending the interpretations are interchangeable. Name a specific source or interpreter rather than assigning a single view to all Christians or all rabbis.
- **No us and them.** Never sort the reader and the writer into two groups: not "a Jew says," not "your church," not "where you already say it." Describe the practice, not the people: "in Jewish prayer," "in every synagogue," "still said in Hebrew today." The Gospel or New Testament line is labeled *Other mentions*.
- **One idea per post.** Keep the daily reflection focused. A study companion can compare two readings when that is the point.
- **The letters and the numbers: wonder, as the tradition's play.** The register is Lawrence Kushner's *Book of Letters* and Daniel Lapin's *Buried Treasure* — each letter a shape with a story, each root a family of meanings, a Hebrew word as how God's language sees the thing. State the tradition's claims as the tradition's ("the rabbis said God created the world with these letters" — Berakhot 55a on Bezalel; Bereshit Rabbah on the *bet*), never as Evan's own fact-claims. Gematria the rabbis made (*sulam* and *Sinai*, both 130, Bereshit Rabbah 68:12; *nachash* and *mashiach*, both 358), acronyms they read (*mizbeach*, Ketubot 10b), enlarged and dotted letters, re-vowellings (*banim* / *bonim*): all welcome in a reflection, told as their reading, sourced, with delight, and never offered as proof. Readers love them; the bank marks which ones are classical and which are later plays, and the post says so too. A root picture is a device, not a requirement; never force an etymology to get one.
- **Never salesy.** The paid line is a door, not a wall. Every free section is a complete gift. Avoid “unlock,” “exclusive,” “don’t miss,” and invented scarcity in editorial copy. Keep actual terms intact in Substack’s existing offer and billing templates.
- **AI-smell list (strike on sight):** framework, journey, navigate, leverage, unlock, delve, tapestry, "here's the thing," "let's dive in," announced connections ("this brings us to"), stacked adjectives, three-beat escalations.

## Current editorial decisions

- Keep One Word Wiser and the approved short description above.
- Use a clear, compelling human title when it fits. Evan’s approved example is **You Can Still Come Home**. Keep Hebrew visible in the body; a subtitle may carry the word when Evan chooses one.
- No fixed application slogan and no “secret” product promise. Content-specific headings make the familiar shape feel fresh.
- The weekly study companion in `study-companion/README.md` is a proposal, not a live benefit.
