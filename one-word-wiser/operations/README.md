# One Word Wiser operating system

September 14, 2026. Scope: the whole publication. Prepared for use on request; no unattended scheduler or cross-app dispatcher is enabled by these files.

## The purpose

Grow a trusted daily publication about Hebrew and Jewish wisdom, especially for Christians and other curious readers. Each letter teaches something accurate and useful, then addresses a recognizable human need. The reader experience stays elegant and familiar. Behind it, we learn to use AI well through real work.

Keep the current audience, cadence, paid offer and approval rules in the positioning, format and monetization documents. An experiment does not change a subscriber promise. “Conversion” here means subscribing or upgrading, not changing religion.

## One repeatable loop

**Choose → gather evidence → make → review → revise → approve → deliver → learn.**

1. Choose one clear reader need and Hebrew word. Check the word ledger and recent letters for repetition. Bring Evan a recommendation with reasons.
2. Retrieve relevant material from Evan's Evernote and verified Jewish sources. Personal archives provide leads and voice; verify publishable quotations against their sources. Keep private pastoral, family and financial material out of shared production records.
3. Draft using the existing One Word Wiser skill. Prepare only the accompanying assets needed for the current task. Reuse approved templates.
4. Review the actual artifact against the skill's `references/evals.md`. Record pass, fail or pending with evidence. Re-read as a reviewer after drafting; do not call that an independent review. Use a second reviewer only when available and authorized.
5. Fix failures and recheck affected criteria. After two revision passes, surface the unresolved issue with a recommended resolution. Human taste remains Evan's job; an evaluator's pass is not editorial approval.
6. Present one review packet: recommendation, final copy, source notes, remaining decisions and exact next action. Preserve materially different earlier drafts.
7. Execute the currently authorized action. Record the saved URL and actual state. A local draft, saved draft, scheduled letter and delivered email are different states.
8. After comparable observation windows, record results and one lesson. Update the relevant template or skill only when evidence or Evan's explicit correction warrants it. Link the example that supports the change.

## Three loops, one small record

| Loop | When | What it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Quality | Every letter or asset | A reviewed artifact with evidence and unresolved checks |
| Growth | Weekly review, when requested | One recommendation from comparable results and one next experiment |
| Tool learning | One bounded experiment at a time | Something useful, what we learned, and whether to keep using the tool |

A tool experiment can be worthwhile because it teaches Evan and the agents a useful capability, even before it improves conversion. Record learning value separately from growth evidence. Do not roll out several new tools simultaneously: we need to know what helped.

## Roles and handoffs

Evan sets direction and approves editorial and product decisions. Grok is the preferred chief of staff: priorities, open decisions, follow-up and summaries. Claude and Codex research, draft, review and implement. Claude Design handles reusable layouts; Magnific handles a specific media request when needed; Hermes can operate an authorized established workflow.

These are roles, not a claim that the bots are already connected or running. The current available agent can perform a role. Every handoff names the repository path and revision, requested output, acceptance criteria, current authority and destination for the result. Never keep competing master instructions in each app. Pull first; commit and push changes. Do not put credentials or raw Evernote archives in Git.

## Four requests Evan can use

- **“Prepare the next letter.”** Read the ledger and current drafts, recommend a word, gather sources, draft and return a review packet. Do not schedule automatically.
- **“Review this draft.”** Apply the evaluation checklist to the specified revision, fix authorized editorial issues and report remaining checks.
- **“Run the weekly growth review.”** Collect available publication-level aggregate metrics, compare equivalent windows, recommend one change, and create a cycle record from `cycle-template.md`.
- **“Try this tool on a real task.”** Choose a small useful output, define success and a time/cost limit, perform the available authorized steps, inspect the result, and record what to repeat. External spending requires current authority.

The agents manage the files; Evan does not need to operate a command line.

## Measurement that can teach us something

Record publication timezone, source URL or export, observation time, post age and window. Compare at 72 hours and seven days where available. Track attributed free subscriptions, mature paid starts, unsubscribes and their denominator, meaningful responses, and production/review effort. Track cancellation/retention over an appropriate longer window when available. Use aggregate data without subscriber identifiers.

Unavailable is not zero. Views, opens and likes are supporting signals, not subscription attribution. Report raw counts with rates and denominator. Do not infer paid demand from the all-free launch. One successful post suggests a hypothesis; it does not prove which edit caused growth. Keep recurring operational improvements separate from changes to editorial taste or paid benefits.

## Why this design

Peter Yang's [June 3 tutorial on skills, evaluation and memory](https://creatoreconomy.so/p/full-tutorial-build-self-improving-claude-skills-in-20-min) distinguishes improving a current output through evaluation from improving future work through concise lessons. His [learning-by-solving-real-problems essay](https://creatoreconomy.so/p/the-fastest-way-to-learn-ai-is-to-solve-your-own-problems) supplies the practical learning approach. These are adapted principles, not a claim of autonomous self-improvement or a verbatim implementation of his software.
