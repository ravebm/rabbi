# חַיִּים

*chayim* (chah-YEEM) — life

</div>

Say it once. *Chayim.* You've probably said it before, at a wedding, with a glass in your hand: *l'chayim*, to life.

Here's the thing nobody mentioned at the wedding. The word is plural.

## Things You Can't Count

*Chayim* has the same ending as *mayim*, water, and *shamayim*, sky. Hebrew puts a few things in the plural that English keeps singular, and they're the things you can't count. You can't have one water. You can't have one sky. And in this language you can't have one life.

## One Key Verse

> **"I have set before you life and death, blessings and curses. Now choose life, so that you and your children may live."** — Deuteronomy 30:19 (NIV)
> *...u'vacharta ba**chayim**, l'ma'an tichyeh atah v'zar'echa.*

The word under "life" is *chayim*. Plural. And the "you" who chooses is singular. One person, choosing something that can't be held alone. And the verse doesn't stop at you: *you and your children.*

## The Kind of Thing That Spills

So "choose life" doesn't mean choose your life. The Hebrew won't let it. It means choose the kind of thing that spills over: the meal that feeds more than you, the word that gives someone else room, the decision made with your children in the sentence.

## Let It Change Your Life

*But there is one line added to Jewish prayer only during these ten days, between Rosh Hashanah, the New Year, and Yom Kippur, the Day of Atonement. It asks God to write us in a book. Which book? And why can't you ask it for yourself alone?*

`[PAYWALL]`

For ten days a year, one line is added to the prayer said three times a day in every synagogue. It's short. *Zochreinu l'chayim, Melech chafetz bachayim, v'kotveinu b'sefer hachayim, l'ma'ancha Elohim chayim.* "Remember us for life, King who desires life, and write us in the book of life, for your sake, living God."

Read the Hebrew again. *Chayim* four times in one breath, and every one of them plural. The book God is asked to write in is not the book of my life. It's *sefer hachayim*, the book of lives.

You cannot ask to be written into the book of lives alone. The prayer has no singular form. You go in with everyone, or the sentence doesn't work.

<p style="text-align: center">— <strong>ח</strong> —</p>

That's the secret of these ten days, hidden in a plural ending. The season feels like a private audit: my year, my wrongs, my name in the book. The prayer refuses. It's *us*, and it's *lives*, and the King who is asked to do the writing is described as one who *wants* them.

*The book of life is a book of lives. Put someone else's name in it tonight.*
