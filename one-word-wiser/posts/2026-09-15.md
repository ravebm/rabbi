# שְׁמַע

*shema* (sheh-MAH) — hear

</div>

Say it once, out loud. *Shema.* Two syllables, the second one open, like a door.

It's the first word of the sentence at the heart of Jewish prayer, said every morning and every night. Many people have said it as their last words. When Jesus was asked which commandment comes first, this is the sentence he answered with.

> **"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one."** — Deuteronomy 6:4 (NIV)
> ***Shema** Yisrael, Adonai Eloheinu, Adonai echad.*

The word under "hear" is *shema*. And here is the surprising part. Biblical Hebrew has no separate word for "obey."

*Shema* means both. A few chapters later, the NIV translates the same verb as "obey": a son "who does not obey his father" (Deuteronomy 21:18). In the Hebrew, that is a son who does not *shema*. Not a deaf son. A son who heard and didn't do anything.

English pulled hearing and obeying apart a long time ago. We hear things all day. We act on almost none of them. Hebrew never let the two separate. To hear, in this language, is to let the sound reach your feet.

So the first commandment isn't a belief. It's an instruction to the ears. Before you love, before you do anything, listen in the way that changes what you do next.

Then comes the very next verse: "Love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength." Hear, then love. The order is the teaching.

*Other mentions:* Mark 12:29, where Jesus quotes this verse as the first commandment.

*But in every Torah scroll, two letters of this verse are written larger than all the others. Scribes have copied them that way for many centuries. The Jewish sages said the two letters spell a word. What is the scroll trying to tell you?*

`[PAYWALL]`

Open any Torah scroll to this verse and two letters are the wrong size. The last letter of *shema*, an *ayin*, and the last letter of *echad*, "one," a *dalet*, are written large, taller than every letter around them. The scribe does not get to choose.

Pull those two letters out and set them side by side and they spell a word: *ed*. Witness.

A fourteenth-century commentator, the Baal HaTurim, said it plainly. Every time you say the *Shema*, the scroll is saying that you are a witness. Not a believer. A witness. Someone who has seen something and is now on the record.

That changes what the sentence is for. "Hear, O Israel" is not a creed you hold. It's testimony you give, twice a day, with your own mouth. And a witness who has heard is bound by what they heard.

*You are not asked to believe the sentence. You are asked to witness it.*

Tomorrow: *selichah*, forgiveness.

— Rabbi Evan
