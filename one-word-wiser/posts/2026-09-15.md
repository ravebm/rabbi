# שְׁמַע

*shema* (sheh-MAH) — hear

</div>

Say it once, out loud. *Shema.* Two syllables, the second one open, like a door.

It's the first word of the sentence Jewish prayer opens and closes every day with, and the last words many people have said before they die. When Jesus was asked which commandment comes first, this is the sentence he answered with.

> **"Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one."** — Deuteronomy 6:4 (NIV)
> ***Shema** Yisrael, Adonai Eloheinu, Adonai echad.*

The word under "hear" is *shema*. And here is the thing about it: biblical Hebrew has no separate word for obey.

*Shema* is both. A few chapters later the same translation renders the same verb "obey": a son "who does not obey his father" (Deuteronomy 21:18) is, in the Hebrew, a son who does not *shema*. Not a deaf son. A son who heard and didn't move.

English pulled those two things apart a long time ago. We hear things all day. We obey almost nothing we hear. Hebrew never let them separate. To hear, in this language, is to let the sound reach your feet.

So the first commandment, in the sentence Jesus called the greatest, isn't a belief. It's an instruction to the ears. Before you love, before you do anything, listen in the way that changes what you do next.

Then the very next verse: "Love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength." Hear, then love. The order is the teaching.

*Other mentions:* Mark 12:29.

*Hearing, in Hebrew, is something you do with your feet.*

*The secret inside shema:* what two oversized letters in every Torah scroll are spelling behind your back, and what God is asking of the person who heard something this week and hasn't moved yet.

`[PAYWALL]`

Open any Torah scroll to this verse and two letters are the wrong size. The last letter of *shema*, an *ayin*, and the last letter of *echad*, "one," a *dalet*, are written large, taller than every letter around them. They have been written that way for as long as anyone has been copying scrolls, and the scribe does not get to choose.

Pull those two letters out and set them side by side and they spell a word: *ed*. Witness.

A fourteenth-century commentator, the Baal HaTurim, said it plainly: every time you say the *Shema*, the scroll is saying that you are a witness. Not a believer. A witness. Someone who has seen something and is now on the record.

That changes what the sentence is for. "Hear, O Israel" is not a creed you hold. It's testimony you give, twice a day, with your own mouth. And a witness who has heard is bound by what they heard.

That's what the first commandment asks. Not agreement. Movement.

You know the one. The thing someone told you, plainly, that you've been treating as information. It wasn't information. It was testimony, and you're the one who heard it.

So today, when you tell someone you heard them, do what the Hebrew means: let it reach your feet. One act, before bed, that proves the hearing happened.

*You are not asked to believe the sentence. You are asked to witness it.*

Tomorrow: *selichah*, forgiveness.

— Rabbi Evan
