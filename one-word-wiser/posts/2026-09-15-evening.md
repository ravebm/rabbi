This morning, *shema*, hear, asked us to leave room for another person's answer.

*You can bring your divided heart to the One who asks for all of it.*

`[PAYWALL]`

Put your finger on the verse after this morning's reading. Deuteronomy 6:5 asks us to love God with all our heart. Leave your finger on “all.”

[Rashi, commenting on those words](https://www.sefaria.org/Rashi_on_Deuteronomy.6.5.2), understands them to include both inclinations: the impulse toward good and the impulse toward evil. Both belong to the heart being asked to love God.

That is a demanding reading. I hear an invitation to bring even the unruly part of ourselves into service. The part that wants to win cannot simply be left outside while the better part goes in to pray.

Imagine the effort you spend defending your position. What could that effort become if you gave it to understanding the other person? That energy is already there, waiting for direction.

Rashi is reading a command to love God. I carry his reading into a conversation this way: the whole heart has work to do, including the part that would rather interrupt.

You know the conversation that followed you into the kitchen tonight. You have improved your argument several times since it ended, while the other person's voice has become quieter in your memory. There may be something left to hear, even if you still disagree.

Tomorrow, give that person one question you do not already know the answer to, and let them finish their answer.

*Let the part of you that wants to win help you listen longer.*

*Laila tov* — good night. May your whole heart find rest. Tomorrow: *selichah*, forgiveness.

— Rabbi Evan
