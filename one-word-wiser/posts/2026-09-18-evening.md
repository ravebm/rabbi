This morning, *chayim*, life, asked us to consider who will live with our choices.

*There is room for more than your own name in tonight's prayer.*

`[PAYWALL]`

Open the High Holy Day prayer book to the beginning of the standing prayer. Among its familiar blessings is a request added during these Days of Awe:

“Remember us for life, King who desires life, and write us in the Book of Life, for Your sake, living God.” That is my translation of the petition in the Rosh Hashanah morning service, in the first blessing of the standing prayer.

*Chayim*, life, keeps returning. But listen to the little word beside it: us.

You can stand with your own fear. You can want another year with all your heart. The prayer gives that longing words, and those words place other people beside you.

We ask to be remembered together. The page has no space where I can cross out another person's name and leave only mine. That is the image I carry from this prayer: a book wide enough for names I wouldn't have thought to include.

You know the one: the person whose worries you haven't had room for this week. You have worries of your own, and there may be little you can do for theirs tonight. But you can let them belong in the same sentence as you: remember us.

Tonight, name one person before you say, “Remember us for life.”

*Let someone else's name rest beside yours.*

*Laila tov* — good night. Tomorrow: *havdalah*, separation, as we gather the week's five words.

— Rabbi Evan
