# חֶסֶד

*chesed* (CHEH-sed) — loyal love

</div>

Say it with me: *chesed*, loyal love. The first sound comes from the back of the throat, like the end of Bach. Two syllables, with the first one carrying the weight.

Its root letters are chet, samech, dalet, part of a family of words about kindness and loyalty. Picture someone returning to a friend's doorstep after the first rush of visitors has ended. That is one way to picture *chesed*, loyal love.

Psalm 136 opens with this:

> **Give thanks to the LORD, for he is good; his loyal love lasts forever.** — Psalm 136:1 *(my translation)*
>
> *Hodu l'Adonai ki tov, ki l'olam **chasdo**.*

That last word, *chasdo*, means “his loyal love.” It is *chesed*, loyal love, with an ending that tells us whose love the psalm is praising. Here, the word is held beside a promise of duration: forever.

We often notice love when it arrives: the flowers, the person who drops everything and comes. Those moments matter, but *chesed*, loyal love, also gives us a way to notice what continues after the occasion has passed. Someone remembers that Thursday is the difficult day and calls again.

The psalm praises God's faithfulness and gives us something to practice on a human scale. We cannot promise forever to everyone, but we can keep the promise we already made. The meal we said we would bring still needs bringing, even when the impulse that moved us has faded.

Perhaps *chesed*, loyal love, is easier to recognize in another person than to name in ourselves. Think of someone who has been steady with you, whose care had ordinary details: a ride, perhaps, or a place kept for you at the table. Those details were how you knew you could count on them.

*Love keeps its promises in small, ordinary ways.*

**Today's question:** What small promise of care can you keep today?
