# שְׁמַע

*shema* (sheh-MAH) — listen

</div>

Someone you love says, “You’re not listening.”

You could repeat every word they just said. You were also preparing your answer.

It is a familiar moment. We want to understand each other, but we want to be understood, too.

## A Prayer That Begins with Listening

> **“Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one.”** — [Deuteronomy 6:4 (NIV)](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy+6%3A4&version=NIV)
>
> ***Shema** Yisrael, Adonai Eloheinu, Adonai echad.*

The first word, *shema*, is a command: listen. It gives its name to the prayer recited morning and evening in Jewish tradition.

The Hebrew verb can mean hearing a sound, listening attentively, or heeding what someone says. The context tells us which. Here, it calls Israel to pay attention to God.

The next verse asks us to love God with our whole heart, soul and strength. First listen. Then love. I think that order has something to teach us about the people we love as well.

We cannot know what another person needs if we have already decided what they are going to say.

## Before You Answer

[Pirkei Avot](https://www.sefaria.org/Pirkei_Avot.5.7), a collection of rabbinic teachings, describes a wise person as someone who does not interrupt another person or rush to answer.

I find that a generous place to begin. Wisdom is something we can practice in our next conversation.

Often, the urge to interrupt comes when we feel misunderstood. Someone is explaining why something hurt. We remember it differently. We have reasons, and we want a chance to explain.

Letting them finish can feel difficult. What if they think our silence means we agree?

We can listen and still disagree. We can hear someone’s hurt and still need to say something hard. But first we can make room for an experience different from our own.

Ask what hurt. Listen long enough that you could describe it fairly. A simple “Have I understood you?” gives the other person a chance to tell us what we missed.

Our answer may change. It may not. Either way, we will know more about the person we are answering.

Today, when you notice your answer forming while someone you love is still speaking, set it down for a moment.

*Let them finish first.*

— Rabbi Evan
