# חַיִּים

*chayim* (chah-YEEM) — life

</div>

Say it with me. *Chayim* — life. Let the first sound come from the back of your throat.

The word belongs to the family of *chai*, alive. Picture a person breathing beside you. Before you know their name, before they have done anything for you, there is a life there.

*Chayim*, life, has a plural ending, which Hebrew uses even for one person's life. The grammar alone doesn't tell us how to live together. Today's verse does.

Moses is speaking near the end of his life. The people will cross the Jordan without him. He leaves them a choice:

> **"Choose life, so that you and your descendants may live."** — Deuteronomy 30:19, excerpt *(my translation)*
> *Uvacharta **bachayim**, lema'an tichyeh atah vezarecha.*

Choose *chayim*, life, then look at the next words: you and your descendants. The choice reaches beyond the person making it. Moses asks the people to imagine someone who will live with what they choose, someone who may not even be born yet.

You know how a small choice can travel: someone speaks sharply at breakfast, and you carry the voice into the car. Someone listens without rushing you, and you have more patience for the next person. We leave something behind in the people we meet.

Choosing *chayim*, life, can begin there: before you send the message, imagine the person opening it. You can say what needs saying and still remember that someone has to receive it. Their life continues after your words arrive.

*Chayim*, life, is bigger than the moment in which you get your way. Moses gives you a question large enough to change a very ordinary Friday: what will this choice leave for someone else to live with?

*Choose what gives life a little more room.*

**Today's question:** Who will live with the choice you are about to make?

Tonight: why our prayer for the Book of Life asks God to remember us together.

— Rabbi Evan
