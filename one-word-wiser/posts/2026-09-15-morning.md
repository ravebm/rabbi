# שְׁמַע

*shema* (sheh-MAH) — hear

</div>

Say it with me. *Shema*, hear. Let the last syllable have a little room.

Someone is still talking. You already know what you want to say. You have your answer ready, perhaps even a good answer, while the person across from you is trying to finish.

This word asks you to wait.

The root, *shama*, means to hear; *shema*, hear, is its command form. In biblical Hebrew, hearing can also mean heeding: receiving words and responding to them. Picture someone calling your name from the next room, and your head turning toward the voice.

Here is the verse:

> **“Hear, Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one.”** — [Deuteronomy 6:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.6.4), *(my translation)*
>
> ***Shema*** *Yisrael, Adonai Eloheinu, Adonai echad.*

*Where it echoes:* [Mark 12:29](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark%2012%3A29&version=NIV).

The verse begins with a request for attention. Before Israel says anything about God, Israel is called to listen: *shema*, hear. Even a declaration this familiar has an opening word we can hurry past on our way to the words we already know.

That is easy to do with a person, too. We recognize the subject and stop expecting a surprise: our spouse is worried about work again, or our friend is telling a story we have heard before. We think we know where this is going.

*Shema*, hear, gives us another possibility: stay long enough to discover what is being said today. A familiar worry may have become heavier; a familiar story may be asking for company. You cannot know while you are rehearsing your response.

Let *shema*, hear, interrupt you once today. Give someone the space to finish, including the pause when they are deciding whether to say more.

*Listening leaves room for an answer you had not prepared.*

**Today's question:** Whose next sentence could you make room for today?

Tonight: how can you listen with your whole heart when part of you still wants the last word?

— Rabbi Evan
